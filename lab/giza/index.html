Four ways to read a construction

The rule is ours.
The evidence has a source.

01

Describe a shape

Our loop makes exact squares. A survey starts with surviving, damaged traces and estimates what is missing. An ideal model and a measured monument answer different questions.

Read the survey →
02

Move a material

Merer’s surviving log records river transport from Tura towards Akhet-Khufu. Research on ancient waterways adds environmental context. Neither gives us a complete algorithm for lifting the pyramid.

Read the log and water studies →
03

Coordinate the work

A lower layer comes first in our model. Real work also needs supplies and places to live. Excavated settlement remains reveal parts of that organisation, not a census or a complete schedule.

Read the settlement report →
04

Keep the question open

A ramp at Hatnub is evidence from another quarry. It does not establish the Great Pyramid’s ramp system. A plausible animation is still a hypothesis when the historical evidence does not settle it.

Read the field account →

Six primary sources · checked 04 October 2026

Open the evidence.

01 / 2017 Pierre Tallet · The logbook of Merer

A primary edition of papyri Jarf A and B. Read: bibliographic pages, Annex I translations and opening synthesis. Fragmentary records concern transport; the editor says construction techniques are not described. The complete monograph was not read.

Open primary source ↗
02 / 2022 Sheisha et al. · Nile waterscapes

Original palaeoenvironmental research. Read: abstract, G1/G4 reconstruction and selected methods. Pollen and sediments support a water-level proxy with dating uncertainty, not a daily depth or boat capacity. Supplementary data were not reanalysed.

Open primary source ↗
03 / 2024 Ghoneim et al. · Ahramat Nile Branch

Original research using radar, topography, maps, geophysics and coring. Read: abstract, introduction, conclusion and selected methods in the university PDF. Mapping former river segments is not observing an individual delivery. No raw-data replication.

Open primary source ↗
04 / 2015 AERA · Another Official’s House

Excavators’ report, AERAGRAM 16(2), pp.18–21. Read: article text. Courtyard and kitchen remains are interpreted as an official’s residence linked to provisioning. Partial excavation, chiefly final occupation; not a workforce census.

Open primary source ↗
05 / 2015 Glen Dash · The Great Pyramid’s Footprint

Original survey report, AERAGRAM 16(2). Read: text pp.8–11 and 14; diagrams 12–13 not visually checked. Regression estimates missing corners. This does not establish ancient surveying or ramp methods.

Open primary source ↗
06 / 2018 Roland Enmarch · Hatnub field season

Field co-director’s account, 9 October 2018. Read: complete short report. Clearance exposed a haulage ramp, rock-cut steps and post-holes. Hatnub is another quarry context; this does not establish the Great Pyramid’s ramp layout.
