Foundations of Computing · Architecture & construction · 2026/27

One object per team.
Rebuilt through verifiable data.

The semester works toward a digital twin of a real building: collect the data, sort it into a system, and keep every claim tied to its source. A source is part of the data; what remains unknown stays visible — that is a result too. Classes are held every Monday.

Current step · announced 12 October

Before the next class, every team:

Deadline: by the next lecture · send to ntunionhub@gmail.com

  1. Review the register of teams and themes and report the missing information, so the list is completed: 3esign.github.io/computing-studio/timovi.html
  2. If you have not yet: form a team of 2–3 students, choose an architectural-civil object and a research theme, and start collecting and sorting the gathered data (online and offline · analogue and digital) — build a “mind map” of the collected data: an information system, a meta BIM (building information modelling).
  3. Send a pilot/preliminary version of the digital passport with the data from the first research wave (mostly online), with every fact bound to a concrete source (websites, institutions — a fact with proof of origin).
  4. In parallel, keep collecting data preferably offline: archives, contacts with relevant people, interviews, companies.

2026-10-12Class 02 · consultations

The data map: sixty-five teams brought their first system

Consultations 13:15–15:45. Every team showed its collected material as a data map — a first order among elements: relevance, importance, accuracy, source, hierarchy. The groups have chosen their objects and research tracks; objects reach from Dorćol to Užice, Pančevo, Sremska Mitrovica, Vršac and Podgorica. Agreed next steps: terrain, archives, interviews, e-mail inquiries, AI Studio experiments. One team presented a full question map for the Generalštab building — 86 questions in eight areas.

2026-10-05Class 01

Choosing a topic: how an object becomes data

The worked example of the whole semester was put on the table: the old Sava bridge, rebuilt through the verifiable data of its digital passport — sources, claims, disagreements and unknowns. The groups then chose their own objects and research tracks. The full record lives in Held classes; the example itself became the course pilot, below.

The course pilot · Object 001

The old Sava bridge — a building that exists only where its evidence carries it.

The full digital passport of Object 001, where every measured value in conflict stands side by side: two spans, two widths, two masses — none chosen, all visible. A silent film tells how the object becomes visible; a 3D scene lights each part of the bridge only when its source is present. A pilot record, not a standardised form or a completed building survey.
