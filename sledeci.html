Collecting data.
Curating what we find.

This class works on the objects the groups chose: find sources, record them with author, date and unit, and organise them into a passport of your object. What cannot be found is recorded as unknown — that is a result too.

Data collection · data curation

Three moves, repeated per source.

Class 01 showed the worked example: the old Sava bridge, its passport, its sources and its unknowns. The same three moves now apply to your own object.

  1. Find the source

    Look for original documents, maps, photographs, measurements and attributed accounts about your object. Prefer the source that can be reopened: an archive, an official publication, a dated report. The library shows what a checked source card looks like.

  2. Record it honestly

    Next to every number and claim write: who says it, when, where exactly, and in which unit. Keep reported values separate from your own calculations. A source is part of the data.

  3. Curate into the passport

    Give your object one place — paper or a copy of the model below — and connect each claim to what it describes. Disagreements between sources stay visible, not erased. Unknowns get a question and a next step.

Before Monday

What to bring.

Your phone or a sheet of paper is enough. Bring the name of your object, its research track, and the first sources — a link, a photograph or a written note with author and date.
